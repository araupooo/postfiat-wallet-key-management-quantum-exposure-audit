Quantum-Resistant PFTL Wallet Key Management Assessment

Which end-user wallet keys are exposed to a harvest-now adversary, which stay hidden behind the 160-bit address hash, and how wallets should migrate to post-quantum signatures.

Pinned tree: postfiatd main 0085a1ff885d6a446369cf73a4430f75af22be55. Sample: 250 consecutive validated ledgers 8024063–8024312, fetched 2026-11-13T21:59:30Z from the public read-only endpoint https://rpc.testnet.postfiat.org/. Server: build 1.0.9, network_id 2025, validated ledger 8024312.

Headline finding. Every transacting account in the sample signs with its master key: 25 of 25 accounts exposed the key their address was derived from, in the clear, on the public ledger. No account in the sample used a regular key and none used multi-signature. At the same time, 89 of 90 AccountRoot entries on one sampled ledger-data page never transacted in the sample at all — their keys are still hidden behind the 160-bit address hash. The exposure is therefore total for active wallets and zero for dormant ones, and the fix is rotation plus migration, not secrecy: the data is already public.

Migration verdict

Migrate wallets to hybrid classical + ML-DSA-65 key pairs (+1,952 B public key, +3,309 B per signature, FIPS 204). The master key cannot be changed — rotation means moving to a new account, or to a regular key, or to a multi-signature list — so the migration is phased: (0) rotate exposed keys to fresh classical keys now, (1) ship hybrid wallets, (2) register post-quantum regular keys and signer keys, (3) activate the post-quantum amendment, (4) retire classical signing. After the activation amendment there is no protocol rollback — recovery is forward-only. Full design, costs, and open questions: migration_design.md.
